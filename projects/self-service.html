01 — THE STARTING POINT

Almost every post-order problem still depended on a human.

Specialists had to identify the right item, interpret order state and policy, then perform the resolution manually.

ORDER TRACKING Self-service available
ORDER CANCELLATION Limited self-service Authenticated customers only
LATE / MISSING ORDER Human specialist
DAMAGED DELIVERY Human specialist
QUALITY / PRINT ISSUE Human specialist
DESIGN ISSUE Human specialist
Six-figure
annual order-status, delayed and missing-order cases

Enough volume to justify moving predictable investigation out of assisted support.

The opportunity wasn't another support interface. It was reusable resolution logic.

02 — PROVING THE MODEL

Self-service didn't start with full automation.

The first web MVP let customers investigate the problem themselves and send CARE structured case information.

01 · HUMAN-LED Specialist investigates and resolves.
→
02 · GUIDED SELF-SERVICE Customer investigates; CARE receives context.
→
03 · RESOLUTION SERVICE Eligible cases resolve across channels.
EARLY SIGNAL

Customers did the investigation.

Phone contacts decreased while email contacts increased: an early signal that customers were willing to do more before reaching CARE.

The architectural decision

Make the resolution logic reusable before making every channel clever.

Different interfaces still needed to resolve the same customer problem consistently.

Initial assumptionEach channel could own and evolve its own self-service flow.
What we learnedThe same issue depended on shared context, state, policy, eligibility and recovery logic regardless of whether it started on web, AI or voice.
What I advocated forDesign the shared resolution model first, then translate the interaction model for each channel.
Impact on the productThe work moved from disconnected flows toward reusable service logic that could support multiple channels and future automation.
Beyond the project

I onboarded another designer into the resolution model and established recurring collective critiques so the logic could keep evolving as shared team knowledge rather than a single-designer artefact.

04 — AUTOMATION BOUNDARIES

Automation wasn't the goal. Reliable resolution was.

We weighed customer experience, financial exposure, technical capability and the value of specialist judgement.

AUTOMATE Damaged delivery

The issue is relatively objective, and the cost of reviewing every image can exceed the exposure from occasional incorrect remediation.

Product → Previous action? → Damage → Resolve
DESIGN DECISION

Skip human proof validation when the business exposure is lower than the operational cost of reviewing every case.

KEEP HUMAN Manufacturing / print issue

“It printed wrong” can describe a production defect, a design issue or customer-authored content.

Product → Evidence → Human diagnosis → Correct action
DESIGN DECISION

Preserve specialist evaluation when diagnosis or judgement materially changes the resolution.

05 — MAKING THE LOGIC REUSABLE

Channels entered with different context, but the same resolution rules could sit underneath them.
