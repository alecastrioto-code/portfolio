ARCHIVE 02 · Sunglass Hut · 2022

Homepage behavioural analysis

Using behavioural data to understand why homepage engagement was not translating into forward movement.

Role UX research + product design
Focus Contentsquare, journeys, zoning, segmentation, prioritisation
Archive format Short case · selected evidence
01 — Context

The problem

Homepage performance raised an uncomfortable question: users who spent more time and scrolled more were not necessarily having a better experience. The analysis compared bouncing and non-bouncing behaviour to understand where the homepage was failing to help people move forward.

02 — What I did

The work

  • Segmented bounce vs. non-bounce behaviour across desktop and mobile.
  • Compared load time, time spent, scroll rate, activity and interaction with navigation / search.
  • Used zoning and journey analysis to understand exposure, attractiveness and interaction patterns.
  • Combined the behavioural evidence with Baymard guidance, competitor review and prioritisation.
03 — Selected evidence

What mattered

01

Bounce ≠ low engagement

Bouncing users could spend longer and scroll more while interacting less.

02

Navigation mattered

Non-bouncing users interacted more with navigation and search.

03

Evidence-backed recommendations

Make search more visible, strengthen category entry points and reconsider homepage content order / exposure.

04 — Outcome

What changed

  • Produced an evidence-backed opportunity map rather than treating homepage engagement metrics at face value.
  • Created a prioritised set of recommendations spanning performance, navigation, search and content exposure.
  • Established a repeatable behavioural-analysis framework for ecommerce UX questions.
What this project taught me

More time on page is not automatically better. Behavioural data became useful when it helped distinguish productive engagement from users simply being stuck.
