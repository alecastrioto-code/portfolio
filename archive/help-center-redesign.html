ARCHIVE 05 · Brandsdistribution · 2019

Help Center redesign

Reframing a struggling support experience around channel fit, self-service and information findability.

Role UX/UI Designer
Focus Support strategy, service design, FAQ IA, customer-service research
Archive format Short case · selected evidence
01 — Context

The problem

Customer complaints and poor reviews pointed to rising response / resolution times and a lack of ways for customers to find information independently. The project started as a support problem and quickly became a service-design problem.

02 — What I did

The work

  • Worked with Customer Service to translate feedback and operational pain points into a support strategy.
  • Mapped support scenarios by urgency, effort and the type of response they required.
  • Used the Fogg behaviour model to prioritise self-help while keeping direct support available for higher-motivation needs.
  • Collected FAQ content from Customer Service, then used card sorting and tree testing to define a scalable information architecture.
03 — Selected evidence

What mattered

01

Channel strategy

Different support scenarios mapped to synchronous / asynchronous support rather than one default channel.

02

Self-service hierarchy

FAQ, documentation and contact routes designed as a connected support system.

03

Findability

Card sorting and tree testing used to organise FAQ content around user expectations.

04 — Outcome

What changed

  • Created a clearer support model spanning FAQ, documentation, Contact Us and direct support.
  • Established a scalable FAQ structure grounded in Customer Service knowledge and user validation.
  • Introduced a stronger self-service layer before direct contact.
What this project taught me

Years before working on AI support, the same principle was already visible: the best support channel depends on the job, and findability is part of resolution.
