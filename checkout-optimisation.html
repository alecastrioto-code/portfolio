02 — ECOMMERCE · CRO · BEHAVIOURAL RESEARCH

Turning checkout friction into a measurable product direction.

Black Friday exposed where Sunglass Hut's cart and checkout were failing customers. I combined behavioural evidence with rapid qualitative research to prioritise the right fixes — then used the work to shape a more scalable checkout system.

The challenge

The checkout had many visible problems. The harder job was deciding which ones mattered most.

Users were abandoning carts, looping between Cart and Checkout, missing shipping options on mobile and encountering usability and performance issues. The project needed to turn scattered behavioural signals into a prioritised product direction.

01 — Diagnose

Start with behaviour, not assumptions.

I combined journey analysis, zoning, performance evidence and session replays with rapid qualitative research to understand both where customers struggled and why.

01Journey loops

Repeated movement between Cart and Checkout signalled uncertainty and friction.

02Mobile discoverability

Shipping options were easy to miss when space and attention were constrained.

03Price clarity

Promotion and total-price presentation created avoidable interpretation work.

04Performance

Slow or unstable moments were treated as experience problems, not only technical ones.

02 — Prioritise

Turn findings into decisions.

The value of the research was not the volume of evidence. It was creating enough confidence for Product, CRO and Engineering to agree what should move first.

EvidenceShipping methods were easy to miss on mobile.→

Increase visibility and reduce interaction cost.

EvidencePricing and promotions required too much interpretation.→

Clarify totals, discounts and information hierarchy.

EvidenceCustomers looped between Cart and Checkout.→

Reduce ambiguity and make progression easier to understand.

EvidencePerformance problems amplified friction.→

Keep technical quality inside the prioritised experience backlog.

The decision behind the redesign

Turn a list of fixes into a reusable product direction.

The evidence pointed to a system problem, not one broken screen.

Initial assumptionBlack Friday friction looked like a set of individual checkout defects.
What we learnedThe same uncertainty repeated across shipping, totals, progression and performance.
What I changedPrioritise evidence-backed journey problems and reusable patterns, with technical quality inside the experience backlog.
Impact on the productImmediate fixes shipped while the component direction moved into design-system and cross-brand reuse.
Beyond the project

I onboarded designers across brands into the optimisation method so teams could run behavioural-analysis programmes independently.

03 — Design

Fix the immediate friction without designing another one-off checkout.

The redesign focused on mobile cart cards, shipping visibility and price / promotion clarity, while moving the interaction model toward reusable components.

Craft proof · mobile shipping

Making delivery choice visible earlier.

Behaviour showed that shipping was being discovered too late. I moved delivery choice and arrival information into the purchase decision itself.

Design decision Make delivery choice part of the purchasing decision, not a downstream checkout setting.

Increase prominence, surface arrival information earlier and reduce the need to loop back from delivery to cart.

Result Higher shipping-method interaction · less cart/checkout looping

Micro-craft: cart controls also moved toward larger ~48px tap targets, clearer quantity/remove actions and a more readable product-price hierarchy.

Reduce search

Put delivery and price information where customers naturally make the decision.

Make state obvious

Use hierarchy and interaction feedback to reduce uncertainty while progressing.

Design for reuse

Resolve local friction in a way that could survive beyond one page or brand.

04 — Scale

The project moved from optimisation to system thinking.

What began as a response to Black Friday friction became a foundation for a checkout direction that could extend across brands. Components were integrated into the design system and prepared for broader reuse.
